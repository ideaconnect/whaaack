How it plays

Simple to start. Brutal by the end.

There is one rule and one number. Everything else is the orchard steadily removing your margin for error.

Whack what pops

Two fruit cycle at first, each refilling on a jittered timer of its own, so the pair drifts in and out of step. A third joins in the sixth gear, a fourth in the seventh, a fifth in the thirteenth — then one more every four gears until all sixteen tiles are busy.

Three escapes and out

A fruit you do not reach in time is a strike, and the orchard pulses red at the edges every single time one gets away — never over the middle, where you are still looking. Three of them end the run.

Milliseconds, not points

Your score is exactly how long you lasted. No multipliers, no combo maths — nothing between you and the number.

It never stops tightening

Fruit arrive faster and live shorter every four seconds, and from the thirteenth gear another one joins every four gears. Neither curve levels off: by top speed, three minutes forty-four in, all sixteen tiles are cycling at once. Nobody holds that.

Ranked or just for fun

A casual run needs no account and is played entirely on the device — nothing about it is uploaded. Sign in and a ranked run posts its score to a global and a weekly leaderboard.

Fair when you come back

Take a call mid-run and the clock stops dead. You come back to a two-second countdown, not to a board that has been running without you. If Android closes the app while you are away the run is gone, but the time you had already survived still counts towards your best.

Remove ads

A single purchase, not a subscription. It is tied to your Google Play account, so it comes back on a reinstall or a new phone — there is a Restore purchases button in Settings for exactly that. Full detail in the terms.

Without it, a short full-screen ad may play between runs — at most one every couple of minutes, and never during one. The game tells you before it does, and offers the unlock at the same moment.
