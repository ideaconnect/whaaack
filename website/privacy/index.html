None of the following is sent to our servers. One caveat first, because "on your device" is not quite the whole story on Android: your settings file — which holds the four toggles below and your best time — is included in Android's own cloud backup and in device-to-device transfer, so a copy of it may sit in your Google account. Your sign-in session is deliberately kept out of both.

  • Your sound, music, haptics and parallax settings.
  • Your best time on this device, set by any run — ranked or casual. We never receive it: a ranked run posts its own score to the leaderboard separately, and that posted score is what we hold. For a Play Games account the stored address is the derived one, so that copy contains your player ID. All of it is deliberately excluded from Android cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so a restored backup cannot carry your session onto another phone.

3. Ads, and your control over them

Whaaack! shows ads through Google AdMob. In practice that means at most one full-screen interstitial between runs, capped to roughly one every couple of minutes. There are no ads during a run and none on launch.

Where Google's platform decides a consent form is required — in practice the EEA and the UK — the app shows it before any ad is requested, via the User Messaging Platform, a Google-certified consent management platform operating under the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework. That form is where you allow or refuse personalised advertising, and where you can see every advertising partner involved. Where Google's framework does not require a form, none is shown and no consent is collected.

Two separate rules apply and we treat both as requiring your agreement:

  • GDPR — personalised advertising rests on your consent under Art. 6(1)(a), not on legitimate interest.
  • ePrivacy — storing or reading anything on your device for advertising needs consent under art. 399 of the Polish Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej (ustawa z 12 lipca 2024 r., Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1221, in force since 10 November 2024), which replaced the old art. 173 Prawa telekomunikacyjnego.

You get the game either way. If you allow the device storage that ads need but refuse personalisation, you still get ads — just non-personalised ones. If you refuse everything, the app requests no ads at all. You can change your mind whenever you like: wherever Google's form applies to you, the app's Settings carries an Ad privacy options row that reopens it. If you would rather have none at all, the ad-free unlock removes them.

The app requests the Android advertising ID (AD_ID permission). The Google Mobile Ads SDK also adds the Android Privacy Sandbox permissions (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS) to the app automatically. How Google uses that data is described in Google's own policy.

4. Google Play Games

Whaaack! uses Google Play Games Services for achievements and for the run statistics shown on your Play Games profile. This is worth reading even if you have no Whaaack! account, because it is the one part of the app that sends anything anywhere without one.

It starts by itself

The app initialises the Play Games SDK when it starts, and the SDK signs you in automatically against whichever Play Games profile your device already has — before you have touched anything in the app, and independently of the advertising consent form, which governs advertising only. That first attempt is the SDK's, not ours: version 2 performs it without asking us or you. (If it does not succeed, the app does offer a Play Games sign-in you can press — in Settings, and on the sign-in screen — but those are invitations after the fact, not the thing that starts it.) If your device has no Play Games profile, or you have signed out of it, that automatic attempt finds nothing, no achievements or statistics are ever sent, and the rest of the game is unaffected.

One consequence worth stating, because it is easy to miss: the SDK keeps state of its own on your device as part of that sign-in, and therefore does so before you have agreed to anything. We treat that as part of providing the achievements the app offers, not as advertising storage — and signing out of Play Games is what prevents it.

What we send Google

When you are signed in to Play Games, and only then, the app reports:

  • Achievement unlocks — the four survival milestones. Awarded from the run you have just finished, and also replayed from the best run stored on the device each time the app comes to the foreground and after a Play Games sign-in, so that milestones earned while signed out are not lost. Casual runs count; you do not need an account.
  • A record of every run that ends — how long you survived (in milliseconds and in seconds), how much fruit you hit, how much escaped, the top speed you reached, and whether the run was ranked. Including a run you back out of before it starts, which reports as a zero.
  • Your best run so far — sent alongside every run above, and again each time the app comes to the foreground and after a Play Games sign-in, so a profile is not left showing less than you have actually done, including runs you played before you were signed in.

Each of these is uploaded as it happens rather than batched for later. All of it is attached to your Play Games profile, not to any Whaaack! account, and it is the game's own numbers — nothing about your device, your location or your identity beyond the Play Games profile you are already signed in to. The one field that says anything else is whether the run was ranked, which implies you also hold a Whaaack! account, since only those can play ranked.

Who controls it

Google is an independent controller for your Play Games profile and for everything on it. Their handling is governed by the Google Privacy Policy and the Play Games Services terms, not by this one. We send achievements and statistics to that profile and read nothing back from it — not your friends, not your other games. The single exception is the account-creation flow below, where our backend exchanges a one-time code with Google and uses the resulting access token exactly once, to ask Google who you are. We deliberately do not request offline access, so no lasting key to your profile is issued to us or stored by us.

How to stop it

Sign out of Play Games — in the Play Games app, or in your device's Google settings. From that moment the app sends nothing and unlocks nothing, and the game plays exactly as before: the achievements simply stop happening. There is no switch for it inside Whaaack!, because there is nothing of yours held here to switch off.

To clear what Google already has, use Google's own controls — the route is in section 7, and it works whether or not you ever had a Whaaack! account.

You may also object to this processing under Art. 21 GDPR through the contact form. In candour about what that achieves: we hold no per-player switch to flip, because the sending is decided on your device by whether Play Games is signed in. Signing out is therefore both the faster remedy and the complete one, and it is entirely in your hands. If you object to us, what we can do is confirm the position and help you apply it.

If you use Play Games to create a Whaaack! account

Signing in to Play Games does not create an account by itself. If you choose Continue with Play Games, or accept the offer when you first tap Play ranked, the app asks Google to confirm who you are and we then create a Whaaack! account from that answer. What we receive and keep is your Play Games player ID and your gamer tag, which becomes your starting display name on the public leaderboard — so the name other players see is the one your Play Games profile carries, until you change it in Settings. We receive no email address in this flow, and there is no password. Deleting the account removes all of it from our side; see section 7.

7. Deleting your account — the important bit

Whaaack! has a real delete button, and it really deletes.

In the app: Settings → Delete my account, then type DELETE to confirm. That single action:

  • deletes your authentication user, so the email address is gone — or, for a Play Games account, the player ID and the derived address we held against it;
  • deletes your profile row, so the display name is gone and freed for someone else;
  • deletes every score you have ever recorded, so you disappear from the leaderboards;
  • clears the best score and the sign-in session held on the device, so the account is gone from the phone too. Your sound and display settings stay, and so does the record of an ad-free purchase — that one belongs to your Google Play account, not to this one, and deleting a Whaaack! account does not undo a purchase or refund it.

It is immediate and irreversible. There is no deactivated state, no grace period and no way for us to undo it. Encrypted backups may retain traces for a short period before rotating out; nothing in them is used for any purpose.

Your Play Games profile is not ours to delete. Achievements and run statistics live on your Google Play Games profile, and Google is their controller — so deleting a Whaaack! account removes our records but leaves those untouched, exactly as it leaves an ad-free purchase untouched. You can clear them yourself, and it takes about as long as our own delete button: in the Play Games app → Settings → Delete Play Games account & data, under Delete individual game data, find Whaaack! and delete it — or do the same at play.google.com/games/profile. Google's instructions are here. Deleting a Whaaack! account created from Play Games also does not sign you out of Play Games; if you later sign in again the same way, a fresh account is created, because the player ID is the only thing that identified the old one and we no longer hold it.

Deleting a Whaaack! account also does not stop the reporting described in section 4: for as long as the device stays signed in to Play Games, runs and achievements keep going to your Play Games profile, because that has never depended on having an account with us. Signing out of Play Games is what ends it.

If you cannot get into the app — you lost the device, or you already uninstalled it — use the out-of-app deletion page. It covers Play Games accounts too, which have no email address we could write to; the page explains what we ask for instead.

Deleting your account does not cancel the ad-free purchase: that belongs to your Google Play account, not to your Whaaack! account, and it will still be there if you come back.

9. Children

Whaaack! is not directed at children. In Poland the age at which a child can consent to an information society service on their own is 16 — Poland did not lower the GDPR default. If you are under 16, please do not create an account without the agreement of a parent or guardian; casual play needs no account at all.

The app is not listed in Google Play's Families programme and is not designed for children. If you believe a child has given us data, tell us and we will erase it.

One point specific to Play Games: casual play needs no Whaaack! account, but it is not the same as playing anonymously. If the device is signed in to a Play Games profile, that profile records the achievements and run statistics described above, whoever is holding the phone. A Play Games profile for a child is set up and controlled through Google's own family tools, not through this app — we neither create one nor can we see whose it is. If a child should not be building one, sign that profile out on the device and the app stops sending anything.

10. Security

Traffic between the app and our backend is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). Passwords are hashed by our authentication provider and are never visible to us. Sign-in tokens are stored in the app's private storage and are deliberately excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, so a restored backup cannot hand your session to another device. Access to leaderboard data is restricted by row-level security so no client can read another player's raw records.

11. Automated decision-making

None. We do not profile you and there is no automated decision-making producing legal or similarly significant effects on you within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR. Ranking is an ordinary sort by score.
