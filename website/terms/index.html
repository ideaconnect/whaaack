2. What the service is

Whaaack! is a single-player arcade game. It is free to download and free to play in full, in both editions. Two things are genuinely optional:

  • an account, which is required only to place scores on a public leaderboard — and which, in the Android app, you can create from Play Games instead of an email address, see section 3; and
  • a one-time ad-free purchase, which removes advertising from the Android app.

Neither is required to play, and nothing about the gameplay is locked behind either.

Google Play Games is a third thing, and it is not opt-in. If your device already has a Play Games profile, the app connects to it when it starts — before you have tapped anything — and from then on it unlocks achievements and reports your runs to Google, casual runs included, whether or not you have a Whaaack! account. That is how Play Games version 2 is built: there is no in-app switch, because none of it is ours to hold. Turning it off means signing out of Play Games, in the Play Games app or your Google account settings, after which the app sends nothing and the game plays exactly as before. Exactly what is sent is set out in the privacy policy. All of that is the Android app: the watch edition contains no Play Games code, so none of it happens there.

The Amazfit watch edition

There is a second Whaaack! that runs on Amazfit watches under Zepp OS. It is the same game laid out for a small round screen, on the same account and the same backend, so these terms cover it too. What differs:

  • Nothing in it is for sale, and the game carries no advertising. It is free in full: there is no ad-free unlock, because there are no ads to remove. Its settings page inside the Zepp app links out to three places — a buy me a coffee page, our Android game on Google Play, and our own site — and a tip left through the first unlocks nothing and is handled by Buy Me a Coffee, not by us.
  • You sign in from your phone, not on your wrist. The watch has no keyboard, and the app on it holds no network permission of its own — it asks the watch for the screen size and for local storage, and for nothing else — so creating an account, signing in and out, changing the password and asking for a reset are all done on the Whaaack! settings page inside the Zepp app, under Zepp → Profile → your device → Whaaack! → Settings, and a companion service in that same app makes every network call for the watch. Only an email address and a password work there — see section 3.
  • Watch runs are ranked on a board of their own. They are not mixed with phone scores, because a three-by-three grid tapped with one finger does not produce times comparable to a four-by-four tapped with two thumbs. The account and the display name are shared: one account, one name, two boards — so playing both editions is not keeping two accounts, and section 4 applies to each board on its own.

The watch edition reaches your watch through the Zepp app rather than through Google Play. Zepp Health is not a party to these terms: it sells nothing here and takes no payment for the game. The game, and anything wrong with it, remains ours.

3. Your account

There are three ways to have one in the Android app, and they differ in what you give us:

  • Email and password — you must give an address you control, confirm it, and keep the password to yourself.
  • Google — Google confirms who you are and we receive your address and name. There is no separate password.
  • Play Games — your Google Play Games profile identifies the account. It has no mailbox and no password: internally it is keyed to an address derived from your Play Games player ID, at a domain that cannot receive mail, so there is nothing to send a reset to. Signing in with Play Games again is the only way in — on any Android device carrying your Play Games profile, which is also what makes it portable. Your Play Games gamer tag becomes your starting display name on the public leaderboard, and you can change it afterwards like anyone else. Signing out of Play Games therefore locks you out of this account, since the profile is the only credential; signing back in restores it. Be aware of the limit that follows: this kind of account cannot be given an email address or a password later, and cannot be recovered by us if you lose the Google account behind it. If that happens the account and its scores remain on the leaderboard and we may have no way to establish that they are yours. If that risk matters to you, use an email or Google account instead — both can be recovered.

Only the first of the three reaches the watch. The Zepp settings page signs in with an email address and a password and offers nothing else, so an account held through Google or Play Games — neither has a password of its own — does not sign in there; to be ranked on the watch you would sign up on the Zepp page with an address that has no account on it yet, and that is a second, separate account. Otherwise it is one account throughout: the same profile, the same name, the same password, so a password changed from the Zepp app is the one the Android app then wants. Confirmation and reset links sent from the watch edition open a page on this website, where a new password is typed, so an account can be created, confirmed and recovered by somebody who has never installed the Android game.

Whichever you use:

  • One person, one account. Do not create accounts to inflate or manipulate the board. Note that an email or Google account and a Play Games account are separate accounts even when they are the same person — we have no way to know they are you both times, and we do not merge them. The Android app and the watch edition are the opposite case: one account serves both, so playing on both is not keeping two. Keeping two of your own is not cheating; using several to occupy the board is, and falls under section 4.
  • Your display name is public. It must be 2 to 24 characters long, made only of unaccented letters a–z, digits, spaces, dots, underscores and hyphens, and it must start and end with a letter or a digit. It must also be unique, ignoring capitalisation. Where the name is not one you typed — a Play Games gamer tag, or the name on a Google account — we fit it to those rules automatically: characters they do not allow are dropped, anything longer than 24 is cut, a number is added if the name is already taken, and if too little usable text survives we assign a neutral generated name instead. So the name on the board may differ from your gamer tag, and you are free to change it. Beyond that it must not be offensive, impersonate anyone, contain contact details or advertising, or infringe anyone's rights. We may replace a name that breaks the last rule with a neutral one — every account has to have a name, so there is nothing to delete — and we will tell you why. To report someone else's name, use the contact form.
  • You may change your display name once every 30 days. The first change is always available — the clock starts at your first rename, not at sign-up — which matters if the name you were given was derived rather than chosen. The control for it is in the Android app: the watch edition asks for a name when you sign up but cannot change one afterwards, so if you signed up from the Zepp app and have no Android install, ask us through the contact form.
  • If you are under 16, do not create an account without a parent or guardian's agreement. Casual play requires no account.
  • You can delete your account at any time — see section 10.

4. Fair play

Ranked scores are meant to be earned. You agree not to modify the app, tamper with its traffic, automate play, or submit scores that were not produced by actually playing. We run every submitted run through server-side checks and rate limits, and one the game could not have produced — or one of a flood from a single account — is refused as it arrives; the app tells you the run was not recorded. We can also delete a score after the event, and in serious or repeated cases suspend the account. Where we do that we will say so if you ask.

This holds in both editions, each against its own board: watch runs are checked and rate-limited against limits written for the watch's smaller grid, and one those limits reject is refused as it arrives in the same way. On a watch the submission is made by the companion service inside the Zepp app on your phone, so that link is part of the traffic you agree not to tamper with.

The watch edition has no Delete my account control of its own: it can create an account and sign in to one, but it cannot erase one. After a deletion that page may go on saying you are signed in until it next tries to reach us.

Deletion does not reach your Play Games profile. Achievements and run statistics belong to Google, not to us, and survive the deletion; clear them through the Play Games app or your Google account.
